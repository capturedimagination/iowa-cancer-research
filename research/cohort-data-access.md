# Iowa cohort and case-control data access supplement

Verified October 22, 2026. Purpose: future variable design and feasibility assessment. **No identifying or participant-level data are held, no access application was submitted, and no investigator was contacted.** Archived AHS files are blank instruments/policies and public aggregate tables. A blank questionnaire contains identifier questions but no completed participant responses.

## Agricultural Health Study: essential individual-exposure resource

The AHS is a prospective Iowa/North Carolina cohort enrolled in **1993-1997**. The main study site reports 89,655 participants: 52,394 private applicators, 32,345 spouses and 4,916 Iowa commercial applicators. These are two-state enrollment counts, not an Iowa-only denominator. Minor count differences appear on NIEHS's descriptive page; freeze the actual analytic release rather than assuming these descriptive counts are interchangeable. Active waves occurred in 1999-2003, 2005-2010, 2013-2015 and 2019-2021. Commercial applicators participated in the first two active phases; later active follow-up eligibility narrowed, while passive cancer/mortality linkage continues for all enrolled groups. [Study overview](https://aghealth.nih.gov/about/), [questionnaires and study data](https://aghealth.nih.gov/collaboration/questionnaires.html).

The design can support person/time/pesticide-specific analyses using reported lifetime pesticide use, application practices, farming tasks, smoking, alcohol, medical history and repeated updates. Some ancillary samples and environmental address linkages exist. Exposure algorithms are estimates from reported history, not continuous measured internal dose. Variables and missingness differ by role/wave; obtain release-specific definitions, eligible denominators, proxy-response flags and follow-up/censoring dates. Cancer registry, death, renal and Medicare linkages are different outcome systems with distinct coverage. [NIEHS resource description](https://www.niehs.nih.gov/research/atniehs/labs/epi/resources/data-sharing/ahs).

**Verified access route:** scientific collaboration with an AHS investigator, followed by registration and a proposal/variable checklist in STaRS; Executive Committee review and a confidentiality agreement precede data release. Requests identify variables, years and the institution/research team. Existing specimens/new collection have additional review. Public policy does not promise identifiers or unrestricted linkage rights. Any external geographic enrichment, sharing of derived data or new use must be covered by the approved plan. This is not an open individual-data download. [Current procedure](https://aghealth.nih.gov/collaboration/process.html), [2025 policy](https://aghealth.nih.gov/collaboration/AHS-Policy-2-4-GuidelinesForCollaboration-2025-1.pdf), [STaRS](https://www.aghealthstars.com/).

**Public materials actually archived:**

- `sources/cohort-access/ahs-private-applicator-enrollment-coded-manual.pdf`: 20-page blank coded enrollment instrument; cover OMB expiration 08/31/1996. That administrative expiration is not a data-release cutoff.
- `sources/cohort-access/ahs-public-health-outcomes-2026-10-22.html`: official outcome tables and footnotes.
- `sources/cohort-access/ahs-public-cancer-summary-through-2021.csv`: all 46 rows from public Tables 2a/2b, including each group's `All` summary; role, source and cutoff added. **Cancer registry data are complete through 2021 for both Iowa and North Carolina in these tables.** This does not prove the newest restricted analytic release stops in 2021.
- `sources/cohort-access/ahs-collaboration-policy-2025.pdf` and `provenance.json`: access policy plus dates, hashes and transformations.

The public cancer tables are counts by site and participant group, with overlapping follow-up/DNA/diet subsets. They are neither a person file nor Iowa-only counts; do not sum the subset columns or add `All` to cancer-site rows. Previous cancers at different sites are included. The HTML preserves original denominators and footnotes. [Official public tables](https://aghealth.nih.gov/collaboration/outcomes.html).

## Iowa Women's Health Study: full-cohort access remains to be confirmed

The University of Minnesota describes a cohort of **41,837 Iowa women aged 55-69 in 1986**, designed around diet, body composition and other cancer risk factors. Some original articles report 41,836; retain the chosen release's denominator/exclusions. The baseline driver-license sampling frame and follow-up surveys in 1987, 1989, 1992, 1997 and 2004 are documented in original methods. Survey variables include diet, smoking/activity, anthropometry, reproductive and medical history. Registry-linked cancer and mortality outcomes and some specialized environmental/claims linkages have been used. [University study description](https://www.sph.umn.edu/academics/divisions/epich/research/cancer/), [original linkage methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2917054/).

The official NCI consortium member list links IWHS to **CEDCD cohort id=22**. The descriptive CEDCD page did not expose usable access details in this retrieval. No current public full-cohort download, complete current dictionary, data-freeze date or formal general request policy was independently verified. A university project/contact listing is an inquiry route, not an assurance of access. Record access terms, current custodian and allowed joins as **unverified** until the institution confirms them. The NCI membership itself grants no access to participant records. [NCI membership](https://epi.grants.cancer.gov/cohort-consortium/members/), [official CEDCD link](https://cedcd.nci.nih.gov/cohort?id=22).

An original Medicare linkage paper covers **1986-2004 claims/enrollment**, used protected identifiers for linkage and notes substantial pre-Medicare gaps for younger participants. A later original kidney-cancer analysis follows **1986-2011**. Those are paper-specific coverage periods, not an established final IWHS release date. Existing registry or Medicare linkages do not grant an outside analyst permission to reproduce them or obtain linkage keys. [Claims linkage](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2917054/), [kidney study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33308628/).

**Verified narrow repository route:** dbGaP **phs001905.v2.p2**, CIDR Molecular Pathological Epidemiology of Colorectal Cancer, includes IWHS among multiple source studies. It is a selected colorectal-cancer case set with sequencing, not the full IWHS cohort or an Iowa incidence denominator. The page lists 4,341 consented subjects across studies and 4,518 tumor/matched-normal DNA samples, provides an Authorized Access/Data Use Certification route, and excludes unconsented or insufficient-sample subjects and specified tumor types. IWHS-specific counts, environmental variables, exact diagnosis years and consent groups were not verified. [Official accession](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/projects/gap/cgi-bin/study.cgi?study_id=phs001905.v2.p2).

## Historical Iowa/Minnesota case-control archives

NCI confirms a pooled resource from male NHL case-control studies in **Iowa, Minnesota, Kansas and Nebraska**, described as approximately 1,000 cases and 3,000 controls with detailed pesticide information. Its official page provides an investigator contact route, not a public data-file download or guaranteed approval. NCI's general DCEG policy describes scientific collaboration/data requests and Data Transfer Agreements, including prohibition on reidentification or linking shared files to individually identifiable external records. Availability of each original file, dictionary, residential history or tissue subset requires custodian confirmation. [Official pooled resource](https://dceg.cancer.gov/research/cancer-types/lymphoma-burkitt-hodgkin-non-hodgkin/pooled-analysis-studies-non-hodgkin-lymphoma), [DCEG access policy](https://dceg.cancer.gov/tools/data-sharing).

The original Iowa/Minnesota NHL analysis has **622 white male cases and 1,245 controls**; it covers 1980-1983-era interviews and historical farming/pesticide/work histories. Official IARC documentation gives Iowa diagnosis eligibility March 1981-October 1983 and Minnesota October 1980-September 1982, with specified Minnesota cities excluded. An associated leukemia analysis uses 578 cases and the same-sized control series; do not count these publication samples or later pools as independent populations without a participant-level provenance map. Original sources: [Cantor 1992, PMID 1568215](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1568215/), [Blair 1993, PMID 8427258](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8427258/), [Brown 1990, PMID 2208120](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2208120/), [IARC study documentation](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK396330/?report=reader).

Cases/controls, proxy interviews, recalled exposures, matching factors and historical lymphoma coding require explicit handling. A case-control archive does not by itself supply population incidence rates or reconstruct 2024 exposures. Published numbers cannot substitute for individual records or establish whether a specific requested field is shareable.

## Later Mayo Iowa/Minnesota/Wisconsin series: distinct resource

Mayo's official laboratory page describes a **clinic-based** lymphoma/CLL/Hodgkin case-control series enrolling September 2002-August 2014. Controls attended scheduled general examinations and were frequency matched by age, sex and residence. Public blank questionnaires cover farming/pesticides, main medical/lifestyle factors, female history and diet; blood-derived materials were banked. Later post-August-2014 lymphoma-biobank work is also described and should not silently be pooled with the earlier case-control design. Public study documentation is available, but no general downloadable participant file or current external access terms were verified. [Official Mayo description/questionnaire links](https://www.mayo.edu/research/labs/lymphoma-epidemiology/research/mayo-clinic-case-control-study-non-hodgkins-lymphoma-chronic-lymphocytic-leukemia).

## Before a future model uses any restricted resource

Record the exact release/accession, data freeze, participant/cancer episode unit, study/state/role, shared-control identifiers, entry and censoring dates, exposure recall windows, wave eligibility/response, case definition/histology version, endpoint ascertainment, missingness and allowed geographic resolution. Retain the approved research purpose, institution, agreement/consent conditions, permitted linkage and output-disclosure rules. Public instruments and aggregate tables can guide variable design now; they cannot produce individual exposure-outcome training rows.
