# Iowa cancer evidence: energy, transport, construction and legacy sites

Research snapshot: **October 22, 2026**. This memo and the companion `industry-energy-legacy-evidence.csv` are reusable research records. The CSV keeps the original requested fields and adds IDs, location, pathway, evidence type, publication date, status limits and follow-up data access. A publication date is separate from the period actually measured.

## Findings that change the evidence picture

- **Gasoline fuel has a newer classification than many older summaries report.** IARC classified automotive gasoline as carcinogenic to humans in March 2025, with sufficient evidence for urinary bladder cancer and acute myeloid leukemia. Gasoline exhaust has a separate classification. A hazard classification does not estimate risk from a particular fueling event. [IARC 2025 Q&A](https://www.iarc.who.int/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/QA-Mono-Vol138.pdf)
- **Iowa has historical occupational cancer outcome evidence in nuclear weapons workers.** Keep this separate from nearby-resident risk. [Original-study abstract indexed by EPA HERO](https://hero.epa.gov/reference/1279197/)
- **A June 2026 Atlantic solvent assessment is newly available.** ATSDR evaluated measured PCE contamination and vapor intrusion, finding low risks for the evaluated indoor-air and public-water scenarios. Pre-1993 public-water exposure remains uncertain. The report largely uses environmental measurements from 1982–2019. [ATSDR assessment](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/PCECleaner/PCE-Former-Drycleaner-final-508.pdf)
- **Coal-ash and contaminated-site records identify places to investigate, not observed community cancer effects.** Exposure requires a contaminant, concentration, route, duration and exposed population. Regulatory monitoring wells, finished drinking water, modeled risk and observed cancer are separate data types.

## Evidence vocabulary for later questions and models

| Evidence type | What it supports | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `occupational_outcome` | Cancer incidence or mortality in an identified worker population | Which agent caused an individual case; community effects |
| `environmental_measurement` | A concentration at a specified medium, location and time | Personal dose or number of cancers |
| `risk_model` | Predicted risk under stated exposure assumptions | Observed cases or a confirmed cancer cluster |
| `hazard_classification` | Whether an agent can cause cancer | Iowa exposure prevalence or attributable cancer count |
| `regulatory_status` | Listing, permit, remedy, eligibility or review status | Actual exposure or health outcome |
| `data_access` | Where original measurements or records can be obtained | Findings before those records are analyzed |

Avoid aggregating these types into one severity score. Preserve the numerator, denominator, reference population and follow-up years for any rate ratio. Do not join a site to county cancer rates and interpret the geographic overlap as causation. Worker and resident populations need separate exposure histories.

## Nuclear weapons workers, laboratory workers and commercial nuclear power

### Iowa Army Ammunition Plant / Burlington Atomic Energy Commission Plant

Quella's 2010 University of Iowa dissertation examined 3,889 workers for incidence and 5,743 for mortality. Lung-cancer and male mesothelioma mortality were elevated versus Iowa; overall incidence was lower. The CSV preserves periods and lung-cancer SMRs. External comparisons do not isolate radiation, asbestos, solvents or smoking; registry-era/residency limitations affect incidence ascertainment. Treat this as an original dissertation, not a confirmed peer-reviewed journal publication. [EPA-indexed original abstract](https://hero.epa.gov/reference/1279197/)

NIOSH's Iowa Ordnance Plant record provides SEC designations, technical dose-reconstruction documents and historical worker testimony. Relevant classes include qualifying DOE/contractor Line 1 workers during March 1949–1974 and qualifying radiographers during May 1948–March 1949, with specified workday and employment criteria. Exact legal class wording controls eligibility. SEC status provides a compensation pathway for specified cancers when requirements are met; it is not a finding that every worker's cancer came from the plant. [NIOSH site and original designation links](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/ocas/iop.html)

DOE funds University of Iowa screening for former Burlington Atomic Energy Commission Plant Line 1/Division B workers and Ames National Laboratory workers. Screening includes occupational histories and surveillance for relevant exposures. This is a practical route to records and medical screening, not a census of all plant employees or proof of cancer causation. Program records were updated through September 30, 2025; current eligibility should be checked with the program. [DOE program page](https://www.energy.gov/ehss/former-burlington-atomic-energy-commission-plant-baecp-and-ames-national-laboratory-workers), [UI occupational-history FAQ](https://www.iowafwp.org/line1/faq.html)

Keep this worker evidence separate from ATSDR's **2013 off-site consultation**, which evaluated 2000–2010 surface-water/sediment samples and four private wells sampled in 2009. RDX/HMX exposures were below levels of concern in its evaluated scenarios. It did not evaluate every historical occupational agent, all environmental media or 2026 conditions. [Original ATSDR consultation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/IowaArmyAmmunitionPlant/IowaArmyAmmunitionPlantLHC09192013_508.pdf)

### Duane Arnold Energy Center

As of the research date, NRC records document a restart licensing/review process, including a 2026 restart inspection. The owner describes a targeted 2029 restart. Neither a restart proposal nor an environmental report is an observed cancer study. NRC maintains annual effluent and environmental reports; use the underlying sampling and dose-assessment tables before making claims about releases. No Iowa-specific individual-level cancer study attributing community cancers to this reactor was identified in this search. [NRC facility record](https://www.nrc.gov/facilities-safety/facility-finder/reactors/duan), [NRC annual report archive](https://www.nrc.gov/facilities-safety/operating-reactors/reactor-safety-information-topics/groundwater-contamination-tritium-at-nuclear-plants/radioactive-effluent-and-environmental-reports/duane-arnold), [owner restart FAQ](https://www.nexteraenergy.com/duane-arnold-energy-center/faq.html)

### Creston thorium legacy

A July 2005 NRC/Iowa meeting record identifies buried thorium slag at Fansteel/Wellman Dynamics, Creston. Iowa agreed with the licensee's characterization that the burial met then-current decommissioning standards; institutional controls were maintained. This historical regulatory record is not a measured resident dose or cancer study. Current DNR identity is facility **310817737**, 1746 Commerce Road, Creston. The NRC's similarly named Fansteel/FMRI complex decommissioning project is in **Muskogee, Oklahoma** and must not be merged into the Creston record. [Original NRC 2005 record](https://scp.nrc.gov/reviews/ia_periodic050713.pdf), [DNR facility identity](https://facilityexplorer.iowadnr.gov/FacilityExplorer/SiteDetail.aspx?facID=310817737)

## Coal power, coal ash and municipal refuse fuel

EPA's directory links operator compliance records for Iowa coal-ash facilities, including Ames, Burlington, Lansing, Louisa, M. L. Kapp, Muscatine, Ottumwa, Prairie Creek, Sutherland, Walter Scott and George Neal. Use this as the starting facility universe, then retain unit-level monitoring records. It is not a list of cancer clusters. [EPA directory, updated June 25, 2026](https://www.epa.gov/coal-combustion-residuals/list-publicly-accessible-internet-sites-hosting-ccr-management-compliance)

Ottumwa's operator published a groundwater monitoring/corrective-action report covering **2024**, dated February 26, 2025. The ash pond closed in summer 2023 and remained subject to monitoring. DNR also indexes an Ottumwa-Midland landfill report covering November 2024–October 2025, including listed cobalt/lithium/manganese exceedances. These are monitoring-network findings. They do not identify finished-water concentrations or exposed residents, and source attribution requires background chemistry and hydrogeology. The full DNR download returned HTTP 403 during this search; do not treat its indexed summary as a completed independent audit. [Operator's 2024 report](https://ccr.alliantenergy.com/-/media/aeccr/ccrdocuments/ottumwa/surfaceimpoundment/groundwatermonitoring/ogspondannualgwcarpt2024.pdf?sc_lang=en), [DNR original document endpoint](https://programs.iowadnr.gov/solidwaste/OpenText/DownloadDocument/114848)

A **1988 NIOSH investigation at Ames Municipal Power Plant** directly evaluated worker exposures from coal/refuse-derived fuel operations. Coal-yard dust overexposure was documented; sampled metals were low or undetected. Potential asbestos/silica hazards prompted control and surveillance recommendations. This provides actual historical Iowa workplace evidence, but no cancer endpoint and no current exposure estimate. [NIOSH HETA 86-422-1891](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/171905)

Historical underground coal mines, stone quarries, cement operations and ash disposal warrant separate process records. Coal dust, silica, diesel, asbestos and naturally occurring radon are different exposures. A mine location or historic production map cannot quantify any of them without process- and era-specific information. This search did not establish an Iowa coal-miner cancer cohort or a community cancer effect attributable to coal ash.

## Transport, fuel storage, distribution and petrochemicals

IARC classified diesel exhaust as carcinogenic to humans in 2012, with sufficient evidence for lung cancer and limited evidence for bladder cancer. Relevant tasks include trucking, rail, diesel forklifts, loading docks, heavy construction equipment and underground equipment. An original national trucking cohort followed 31,135 male workers from 1985 through 2000 and associated longer exhaust-exposed employment with lung-cancer mortality. It is not an Iowa-specific estimate; historical fleet technology and smoking adjustment limit direct transfer. [IARC diesel evaluation](https://www.iarc.who.int/news-events/iarc-diesel-engine-exhaust-carcinogenic/), [Garshick et al. original cohort](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2569090/)

Gasoline loading, transfer and storage require attention to vapor exposure, not only engine exhaust. The 2025 IARC upgrade applies to automotive gasoline fuel/vapors; it does not turn all petroleum mixtures into the same exposure. Record benzene separately when measured. Older sources calling gasoline Group 2B are outdated for this agent. [IARC 2025 evaluation](https://www.iarc.who.int/featured-news/iarc-monographs-evaluation-of-the-carcinogenicity-of-automotive-gasoline-and-some-oxygenated-gasoline-additives/)

**Clinton Equistar tank:** ATSDR's June 2008 consultation examined a 500,000-gallon pyrolysis-gasoline tank and benzene monitoring from August 2006/April 2007. Short-term noncancer effects were not expected, but long-term public-health implications were **indeterminate** because the sampling/modeling could not characterize chronic exposure adequately. ATSDR recommended more sensitive long-term monitoring. This is not an observed cancer study or a current-facility determination. [Original consultation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/EquistarPyrolysis/EquistarPyrolysisHC062708.pdf)

**Perry former Chicago, Milwaukee and St. Paul rail yard:** a May 2007 consultation used 2006 soil/groundwater sampling. Arsenic, lead, benzo[a]pyrene and PCBs were evaluated; selected daily employee soil-ingestion assumptions produced potentially concerning modeled risks. The report recommended remediation/controls before reuse. It did not find a cancer cluster, and those pre-remedy conditions must not be labeled current. [Original consultation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/ChicagoMilwaukeeandStPaulRailYard/ChicagoMilwaukeeStPaulRailYardHC053107.pdf)

**Oskaloosa Buchanan Bulk Oil / Ma & Pa Stores:** ATSDR's 2006 assessment describes former petroleum storage and tank-related BTEX/total petroleum contamination. Its conclusion for proposed housing depended on slab foundations and other pathway controls, including avoiding basements. Historical land-use conditions are essential to interpreting the finding. It is not a blanket clearance for all future construction or evidence of citywide cancer effects. [Original consultation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/BuchananBulkOilMaPa/BuchananBulkOilHC093006.pdf)

## Construction, demolition, stone and paving

Respirable crystalline silica is a lung carcinogen relevant to quarrying, concrete/masonry cutting, stone fabrication and sand processing. Iowa DNR's June 2026 Pattison Sand notice documents Clayton County mining/processing activity, but its water-use permit is not an air measurement or cancer study. Occupation, material silica content, dust generation, enclosure/ventilation and respirator use are necessary exposure fields. [NCI silica summary](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/crystalline-silica), [Iowa permit notice](https://www.iowadnr.gov/news-release/2026-06-18/water-use-permits-review-published-06182026)

A University of Iowa-led 2017 study measured air outside 17 homes near sand mining/processing/transport facilities. **The homes were in Trempealeau County, Wisconsin, not Iowa.** Low measured respirable silica and occasional particle spikes in that setting cannot establish Iowa worker or neighbor exposures. CDC repository metadata mentioning Iowa reflects author/institution or regional indexing, not necessarily sampling geography. This is a useful location-check example for future retrieval. [Original full text, methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5600868/)

Asbestos causes mesothelioma and cancers of the lung, larynx and ovary. Renovation, demolition, insulation, repair and legacy industrial maintenance are relevant Iowa processes. Iowa DOT's regulated-materials workflow and DNR demolition/training-fire requirements identify inspection/abatement records to request. They document controls and potential materials, not measured exposure or attributable cases. Capture dates and tasks because latency can span decades. [NCI asbestos](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/asbestos), [Iowa DOT materials records](https://iowadot.gov/transportation-development/location-environment/regulated-materials), [DNR demolition/training-fire controls](https://www.iowadnr.gov/environmental-protection/air-quality/asbestos-training-fires-controlled-burns)

IARC's bitumen evaluation distinguishes oxidized-bitumen roofing exposures (Group 2A) from straight-run road paving and hard-bitumen mastic work (Group 2B). Do not label all asphalt work a proven human lung carcinogen. Product, process and temperature belong in the exposure record. No Iowa-specific paving/roofing cancer cohort was established in this search. [IARC original evaluation announcement](https://www.iarc.who.int/news-events/iarc-monographs-occupational-exposures-to-bitumens-and-their-emissions/)

## Solvents, coal-gas works, landfills and groundwater

**Atlantic PCE former dry cleaner (EPA IAD039954300):** the 2026 ATSDR assessment separates source-well contamination, finished public water and indoor air. It found evaluated indoor-air exposures unlikely to harm workers/residents and public-water exposures since 1993 unlikely to harm users. Modeled PCE excess cancer risks for evaluated air scenarios were approximately 1–2 per million. Pre-1993 water exposure is uncertain. These are modeled findings, not observed additional cases. The source property also held an IDOT laboratory; do not assume the dry cleaner alone caused all contamination. [ATSDR final assessment](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/PCECleaner/PCE-Former-Drycleaner-final-508.pdf)

**Mason City former manufactured-gas plant:** ATSDR's 2008 consultation addressed coal-tar-related BTEX/PAHs/lead and concluded the evaluated site posed no hazard provided land-use restrictions were maintained. Soil treatment/removal and controls are part of that conclusion. [Original consultation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/MasonCityCoalGasificationPlant/MasonCityCoalGasification%20LHC%206-13-2008.pdf)

**Iowa City former manufactured-gas plant:** a 2006 consultation evaluated 1999–2004 measurements at an apartment development with passive venting. Its no-apparent-hazard conclusion depended on evaluated exposure scenarios and land use; changes require reassessment. Legacy coal-gas contamination should not be combined with modern natural-gas distribution as one exposure process. [Original consultation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/IowaCityFMGP/IowaCityFMGP-HC091406.pdf)

**Doty Landfill, Camanche:** ATSDR's 2008 consultation reported arsenic at 14.9 and 19.3 µg/L in private wells sampled in October 2007, above the 10 µg/L federal public-water limit used for comparison. Landfill attribution and duration were unresolved. EPA finalized a substantially updated inorganic-arsenic toxicity assessment in January 2025. Do not recycle an old risk-model conclusion as current reassurance or infer lifetime exposure from one sampling event. [Original consultation](https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/DotyLandfillSite/DotyLandfillSite%20LHC%206-23-2008.pdf), [EPA 2025 IRIS assessment](https://assessments.epa.gov/risk/document/%26deid%3D363892)

The ATSDR Iowa index is a practical expansion route for waste, recycling and solvent sites. Some older assessments are archived or available by request. This memo does not claim that every indexed report was reviewed. A Superfund listing identifies a cleanup problem; it does not by itself identify an exposed population or demonstrate elevated cancer incidence. [ATSDR Iowa document index](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/TSP/PHA/PHAListing.aspx?StateIndicator=IA)

## Next data acquisition and modeling tasks

1. Obtain the full Quella dissertation and construct separate cohort records for nuclear AEC workers and broader Army munitions workers. Preserve smoking information, employment dates, out-of-state ascertainment and latency windows. EPA HERO supplied an indexed abstract; the initially attempted repository PDF did not download.
2. Download every Iowa CCR unit's original annual reports and groundwater tables. Store sampled medium, well ID, coordinates, upgradient/downgradient position, dissolved/total form, detection limit, background threshold, statistic and corrective-action status. Join to private/public-water exposure records only after hydrologic review.
3. For each historical ATSDR site, retrieve EPA remedial decisions, five-year reviews, institutional-control records and the newest sampling. Keep “historical concern,” “remedy installed” and “current exposure known/unknown” as different fields.
4. Request task-specific NIOSH/Iowa OSHA data for quarrying, countertop fabrication, concrete cutting, diesel work, demolition, utility maintenance, waste/recycling and fuel transfer. Cancer-registry linkage requires an occupational cohort and appropriate protections; facility counts cannot replace it.
5. Preserve unavailable data as unknown. For a model, a nondetect needs its detection limit and censoring rule. A regulatory standard is not a zero-risk threshold, and modeled excess risks from different assumptions should not be summed.
