# Synthesis and methods notes

Research cutoff: October 22, 2026 (America/Chicago). Scope: population cancer burden in Iowa, established and suspected contributors, prevention, screening, care access and policy. This is a broad evidence review, not an exhaustive systematic review or an individual diagnosis.

## Current versus historical evidence

The live NCI State Cancer Profiles CSV downloads returned incidence for 2019-2023 and mortality for 2020-2024. Search-indexed previews and the 2026 Iowa annual report often contain older periods. Preserve the live originals and their footers; do not substitute indexed figures. Iowa displayed incidence is 504.1 per 100,000 versus U.S. 453.4, about 11.2% higher. Mortality is 148.2 versus 143.2, about 3.5% higher. These are age-adjusted annual rates, not individual probabilities or a case-fatality ratio. Incidence is based on SEER November 2025 / NPCR 2025 submissions; mortality uses NVSS.

The current incidence CSV displays Iowa second in descending displayed rate but assigns CI*Rank 3 (confidence range 2-3). Its footer explains that displayed SEER rates use 20 age groups and NPCR rates 19, while CI*Rank consistently uses 19. Iowa and West Virginia are close. Use the assigned rank with its caveat; avoid a timeless rank assertion. Iowa's recent incidence trend is rising; Utah's estimated annual increase is larger, so an unqualified 'fastest-rising state' headline is outdated for this table.

## September 2026 drivers project

Official HHS landing page: https://hhs.iowa.gov/health-prevention/cancer

HHS links the Year 1 report at https://iphprp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/FINAL_Cancer_REPORT_2026_JULY_30.pdf . Direct requests returned 403 on the research date. No claim of full-text review is made. The accessible Iowa Medical Society clinical summary states that evaluated factors account for approximately 76% of excess burden among the FIVE modeled cancer types, including demographic, behavioral, radon and UV variables: https://www.iowamedical.org/cancer-resources/ . This is not 76% of all cancer diagnoses, not necessarily 76% of all cancer-type excess, and not proof of individual causes. No original peer-reviewed methods paper for the final analysis was independently verified in this review.

The February interim report is indexed and available at https://iphprp.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Interim-Progress-Report-Cancer-Driver-Project-Feb-2026-final-.pdf . It describes adult 2022 excess calculations and a comparable-state cluster of Minnesota, Nebraska, North Dakota, South Dakota and Wisconsin. Do not automatically transfer its denominator to every September estimate. Its limitations include self-reported BRFSS, incomplete exposures and later planned environmental/genetic work.

## Geography and causation

Iowa HHS explains that cancer counts can include multiple primaries in one person, geography is residence at diagnosis, prior addresses and personal exposures are unavailable, and small counts are unstable: https://hhs.iowa.gov/about/data-reports/cancer-data . Registry maps describe burden; they cannot assign cause. CDC's May 2026 cancer-pattern guidance requires defined populations, time periods, related cancer types, exposure plausibility and latency: https://www.cdc.gov/cancer-environment/php/guidelines/characteristics.html . Multiple comparisons and changing boundaries can create misleading apparent clusters.

## Policy sources

The 2023-2027 Iowa Cancer Plan is a collaborative framework, not demonstrated completion of its goals: https://canceriowa.org/iowa-cancer-plan/ . UI's appropriation narrative confirms the $1 million project and six original target sites (including HPV-associated sites): https://budget.fo.uiowa.edu/special-purpose-appropriations-budget-narratives/cancer-assessment-narrative . Distinguish these six targets from the FIVE cancer types in the 76% modeling summary.

IDALS's June 1, 2026 statement says HF2771 Farm to Faucet provides nearly $320 million over TWELVE years, not a one-year cancer-research appropriation, with a $25 million Central Iowa Water Works infrastructure allocation. https://iowaagriculture.gov/news/farm-faucet-water-quality-bill-sign . Infrastructure investment is not yet evidence of reduced cancer outcomes.

## Inherited risk and screening interpretation

NCI estimates 5-10% of cancers are associated with harmful inherited changes; this is not the share of cancers involving DNA damage, and the rest are not all pollution-caused. https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/genetics/genetic-testing-fact-sheet . Shared family exposures can coexist with inherited risk.

NCI distinguishes detection accuracy from clinical benefit for multicancer blood tests and notes unresolved mortality outcomes: https://prevention.cancer.gov/research-areas/networks-consortia-programs/csrn/q-a-about-mcd-tests . Such tests should not replace established site-specific screening on the strength of marketing claims.

## Limits

This compilation does not test a household's water or air, infer anyone's medical history, establish a specific cancer cluster, audit all industrial sites, or quantify an Iowa-wide attributable fraction for nitrate, pesticides or PFAS. It preserves contradictory/null findings and identifies where measured exposure and historical residential data are needed.
