# Iowa manufacturing, materials and cancer: expanded evidence

Research/access date: October 22, 2026. Scope: machinery, foundries, metal finishing, rubber/plastics, wood/RV components, printing/packaging, batteries/electronics, tanning and medical sterilization. Primary records are emphasized. Historical measurements below are not descriptions of present operating conditions. No record reviewed here establishes that a named facility explains Iowa's statewide excess or caused nearby residents' cancers.

## Most useful new findings

1. Iowa has **direct, historical workplace measurements**, beyond general hazard lists: Keokuk Steel Castings (silica/formaldehyde), Sheller-Globe Keokuk (nitrosamines), John Deere Dubuque (coolant/oil mist) and Winnebago Forest City (wood dust).
2. **Two Iowa occupational cancer case-control studies** identify manufacturing and repair job signals. These are leads for better exposure-specific studies, not proof of causation at named employers.
3. Medical-device sterilization is an Iowa-specific chemical-exposure sector: American Contract Systems operated an ethylene-oxide facility in Grimes; EPA reports controls installed in 2021, while the current state WARN log records a 2026 closure notice. Do not describe it as a verified current uncontrolled source.
4. Site assessments can be reassuring as well as concerning. Alcoa's sampled homes, American Packaging's historical air model, Allied Equipment's proposed commercial redevelopment and capped Mid-America Tanning all received limited-scope no-harm/no-completed-pathway conclusions. Their old dates and exposure assumptions matter.

## 1. Machinery and metalworking fluids: John Deere Dubuque

NIOSH HETA **85-510-1937**, November 1988, investigated coolant concerns and a suspected lung/brain cancer excess. March 1988 air sampling found oil mist **0.11-1.57 mg/m3** (mean 0.38), below the then-used 5 mg/m3 limit; aldehydes and nitrosamines were not detected. The report emphasized that the oil-mist standard did not address possible carcinogenic effects.

Death-certificate review revised the union's suspected cases to **seven lung-cancer deaths and two primary brain-cancer deaths in 1986-1987**. Seven lung deaths did not exceed the report's rough expected number. The two brain deaths exceeded the rough expectation, but one worker was an engineer in a separate research building; a common exposure was not established. No additional case ascertainment or full plant mortality study was conducted. This is neither a confirmed occupational brain-cancer cluster nor comprehensive proof of no long-term risk.

Primary report, summary PDF pp.3-4 and discussion pp.11-13: https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/hhe/reports/pdfs/85-510-1937.pdf . Archived locally.

For wider context, the UAW-GM cohort in **Michigan**, not Iowa, followed tens of thousands of workers exposed to different metalworking-fluid classes. Its 2020 mortality update and 2022 incidence analysis found selected site-specific associations, many null results and healthy-worker/exposure limitations. Formulation and era matter; do not label every modern coolant equally carcinogenic. Original studies: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7737797/ and https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10310982/ .

## 2. Foundries and metal casting: Keokuk

NIOSH **80-081-1173**, August 1982, reports an environmental/medical survey **March 10-12, 1981**, followed by a **medical** survey in March 1982 at Keokuk Steel Castings. Two shakeout respirable-silica measurements were **0.10 and 0.12 mg/m3**, above the report's NIOSH recommendation of 0.05. Eight core/mold-area formaldehyde samples ranged **0.18-0.50 mg/m3**. NIOSH found silica and carbon-monoxide overexposure hazards and recommended minimizing formaldehyde. Aromatic amines in pouring samples were below detection; MDI, hydrogen cyanide, trace metals and ammonia were within the comparison standards then used. The medical component addressed symptoms and lung function, **not cancer incidence**. Primary summary: https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/hhe/reports/pdfs/80-81-1173.pdf PDF p.3. Archived locally.

IARC's evaluation of occupational exposure during **iron and steel founding** is Group 1, based on lung-cancer evidence. Relevant processes include sand molds, shakeout/fettling, hot binder decomposition and metal fumes. Founding is an exposure circumstance, not a statement that every foundry employee has the same dose. Primary evaluation: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK304429/ .

## 3. Welding, plating and metals

IARC classifies **welding fumes** as carcinogenic, causing lung cancer; it reports positive kidney-cancer associations. UV from welding is separately carcinogenic, causing ocular melanoma. This extends beyond stainless steel: mild-steel welding is not exempt. The correct unit of investigation is task, material, ventilation and exposure history. Primary evaluation: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK543200/?report=reader .

NCI identifies occupational cancer risks from **hexavalent chromium** (lung/nasal/sinus), **nickel compounds** (lung/nasal), and **cadmium compounds** (lung). Chromium valence, nickel compound and aerosol/route matter; a solid alloy or trace environmental detection is not equivalent to a high inhalation exposure. Sources: https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/chromium ; https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/nickel ; https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/cadmium .

**Electro-Coatings, Cedar Rapids:** the 1994 EPA/DNR Record of Decision documents chromium contamination from a leaking chromic-acid tank discovered in 1976 and groundwater concerns. It describes plating operations and later investigation of VOCs, with uncertainty over contributions from adjacent Hawkeye Rubber. A 2012 EPA settlement documents hazardous-waste handling violations and a waste-reduction project; it is not a cancer study. Do not convert historical contamination or a penalty into an attributable cancer count. Primary records: https://nepis.epa.gov/Exe/ZyPURL.cgi?Dockey=910022KA.TXT and https://www.epa.gov/archive/epapages/newsroom_archive/newsreleases/e2ee6cc25cf5d3d685257a470062b804.html .

## 4. Aluminum: do not confuse rolling mills with smelters

IARC's Group 1 **aluminium-production** evaluation concerns electrolytic reduction and casting into ingots, with PAH-rich historical exposures; it explicitly **excludes alloying and fabrication of sheet, wire and foil**. EPA describes Davenport Works as a sheet/plate rolling mill. It is not valid to assign the smelter-specific cancer finding automatically to that mill. Process-specific evidence is needed. Sources: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK304404/ and https://www.epa.gov/ia/aluminum-company-america-alcoa-and-mississippi-river-pool-15-superfund-sites-bettendorf-scott .

**Alcoa-Davenport/Riverdale vapor assessment, May 2005:** IDPH/ATSDR evaluated September 2004 samples at two then-vacant adjacent homes. VOCs included PCE, TCE and vinyl chloride; measured indoor/outdoor concentrations were judged unlikely to harm health using that assessment's benchmarks. The report explicitly could not determine prior exposure levels from one sampling event. Its old toxicity values and selected homes must not be represented as a 2026 neighborhood-wide clearance. Primary conclusions PDF p.11; historical-exposure limitation pp.10-11: https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/Sub-SlabGasandAirSamplingData08/SubSlabGasAirSamplingAlcoaHCFinal052005.pdf . Archived locally.

## 5. Rubber and tire production

IARC classifies **occupational exposures in rubber manufacturing** as Group 1, with sufficient evidence for leukemia, lymphoma, bladder, lung and stomach cancer. The evaluation includes tire/general-rubber manufacture and retreading, but not synthetic-polymer chemical production. Changing mixtures and historical aromatic amines, nitrosamines and fumes make modern task-level exposure assessment important. Primary evaluation: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK304412/ .

**Sheller-Globe, Keokuk:** NIOSH **82-156-1231**, November 1982, sampled during July 13-15, 1982. Total nitrosamines averaged **3.6 micrograms/m3** in personal samples, including several identified nitrosamines. NIOSH concluded a nitrosamine exposure hazard existed and recommended ventilation/design improvements and lowest-feasible exposure. This measured exposure study is stronger evidence of an actual historical Iowa hazard than a generic facility name or emissions permit, but it did not count cancers. Source: https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/173078 ; PDF https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/173078/cdc_173078_DS1.pdf . Archived locally.

The 1989 NIOSH tire-worker respiratory surveillance report includes Armstrong and Firestone plants in Des Moines among 21 participating plants. Its radiograph/pneumoconiosis question is **not a plant-specific cancer result**. https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/171840/cdc_171840_DS1.pdf .

## 6. Plastics, resins and chemical intermediates

**Styrene** is IARC Group 2A, with limited human evidence for lymphohematopoietic malignancies plus animal/mechanistic evidence. Iowa HHS identifies plastics/resin workers as potentially exposed. Neither the presence of a plastic product nor a styrene-emitting process establishes that residents received a carcinogenic dose. IARC primary summary: https://publications.iarc.who.int/_publications/media/download/5823/52115fcc9bd21c4a208aa57cb3cc859c909b499f.pdf ; Iowa exposure guidance: https://hhs.iowa.gov/health-prevention/providers-professionals/toxicology/toxicology-manual/s .

**Vinyl chloride monomer** is a human carcinogen, especially associated with liver cancer. NCI explicitly distinguishes monomer from **PVC**, which is not a known/suspected carcinogen. Do not assign monomer-production risk to every plant molding finished PVC or every PVC consumer product. https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/vinyl-chloride .

Petrochemical/pyrolysis gasoline and benzene records for Equistar Clinton are covered by the companion energy/legacy research. Chemical identity, actual use and controls should be checked before grouping all chemical manufacturing together.

## 7. Wood products, furniture and RV components

NIOSH **2000-0250-2837**, April 2001, evaluated Winnebago's Forest City sawmill during September 12-14, 2000. Seventeen personal samples were below the OSHA particulate limit used in the report, but **seven exceeded the 1 mg/m3 NIOSH wood-dust recommendation**, including hand routing, sanding and trimming. NIOSH concluded a potential health hazard and recommended dust-capture improvements. It investigated respiratory symptoms, not cancer rates. The complete September 13 table is transcribed into `winnebago-2000-personal-wood-dust.csv`: **12 total-particulate and five respirable-particulate samples**, with sample IDs, departments, minutes and fraction preserved. The 1.9 mg/m3 hand-router result is respirable, while the 2.2 mg/m3 hand-router result is total particulate. Do not pool these fractions as identical dose measures. Summary PDF pp.4-5; Table 1 PDF p.15 (printed p.9): https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/hhe/reports/pdfs/2000-0250-2837.pdf . Archived locally.

NCI identifies strong associations of occupational wood dust with nasal/sinus cancer. Wood dust, formaldehyde-releasing resins and finishing solvents are different exposure questions and require separate measurement. https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/causes-prevention/risk/substances/wood-dust . Formaldehyde cancer evidence includes nasopharyngeal cancer and leukemia, with variation between original cohorts; do not assume that short-term irritation measures cancer risk. NTP's hazard assessment: https://ntp.niehs.nih.gov/sites/default/files/ntp/roc/content/profiles/formaldehyde.pdf .

## 8. Printing and flexible packaging

**American Packaging, Story City:** IDPH/ATSDR's September 2009 consultation used AERMOD, emission inventories and selected stack tests for six air pollutants, including formaldehyde and benzene. It concluded the modeled outdoor concentrations would not harm community health and recommended follow-up evaluation of planned school monitoring. This was **modeled air exposure**, not measured individual dose or a cancer cohort, and it cannot establish 2026 conditions. PDF pp.4-8: https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/AmericanPackagingCorp9-30/AirEmissionsfromAmericanPackagingCorp9-30-2009.pdf . Archived locally.

The Iowa brain-cancer study below found a historical newspaper-work signal, but that does not identify a printing chemical or prove an employer caused cancer. Modern solvent, coating and ink formulations can differ considerably from earlier jobs.

## 9. Batteries, electronics and other precision manufacturing

Relevant processes can involve cadmium compounds, nickel compounds, chromates, degreasers and specialty dusts. Their use must be confirmed: lead-acid, nickel-cadmium and lithium-ion batteries are different technologies, and not all electronics production involves the same agents. NCI's cadmium and nickel sources above identify relevant carcinogenic exposure routes but do not establish exposure in every Iowa factory. No modern Iowa plant-specific cancer cohort for these subsectors was located in this targeted search. The historical Iowa glioma study reported a female electrical/electronics-manufacturing signal; that warrants exposure-specific replication rather than a blanket electronics-cancer conclusion.

## 10. Leather tanning and legacy manufacturing land

**Mid-America Tanning, Sergeant Bluff:** the February 2005 IDPH/ATSDR review describes former hide processing (1969-1989) and mainly chromium-containing waste, with contaminated soil/sediment capped. It found no current completed exposure pathway under intact caps/fencing; groundwater was not contaminated under the reviewed data. It acknowledged possible earlier worker exposures. The report does not identify every chromium detection as hexavalent chromium or a cancer cause. Its conclusions were conditional on the engineering controls and date. https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/MidAmericaTan021705-IA/MidAmericaTan021705-IA.pdf PDF pp.5-7. Archived locally.

**Allied Equipment, Charles City:** March 2011 IDPH/ATSDR evaluated former manufacturing-associated power-plant land for proposed commercial reuse. Soil exposures were not expected to harm health at that use; groundwater lacked a completed drinking pathway. It recommended plugging an abandoned well and reassessing changed use. This does not reconstruct prior plant-worker exposures or clear residential redevelopment. https://www.atsdr.cdc.gov/HAC/pha/AlliedEquipmentInc/AlliedEquipmentLHC03142011.pdf PDF pp.10-11. Archived locally.

## 11. Medical-device sterilization: ethylene oxide

EPA recognizes inhaled ethylene oxide as carcinogenic; national sterilizer-worker evidence addresses lymphoid/breast cancers. Sources: https://www.epa.gov/ethylene-oxide and EPA's primary risk assessment https://iris.epa.gov/static/pdfs/1025tr.pdf . Iowa relevance is documented: EPA lists **American Contract Systems, Grimes**, and its March 2022 AirToxScreen update says EtO controls were installed there in **2021**. An older uncontrolled-emissions map therefore cannot be assumed to describe later exposure. https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2022-03/2017airtoxscreen-emissions-updates.pdf . The EPA information request independently locates the facility at 1601 Southeast Gateway Drive, Suite 120: https://downloads.regulations.gov/EPA-HQ-OAR-2019-0178-0485/attachment_1.pdf .

**Current-status caution:** the official Iowa WARN log records a January 27, 2026 closing notice for 47 workers, effective March 31, 2026. A closure notice is not direct verification of emissions cessation, and an earlier notice may be amended: do not sum the 2025 and 2026 numbers as distinct permanent job losses or call the site currently operating without further records. https://workforce.iowa.gov/media/1189/download . FDA's October 2025 Grimes recall confirms EO sterilization/re-gassing activity in that period, not worker/community cancer: https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfres/res.cfm?id=216298 .

## 12. Iowa occupational cancer studies and limits

**Bladder, Zheng et al., 2002:** 1,452 cases and 2,434 controls; occupational histories covered jobs held at least five years since age 16. Male associations included rubber/plastics **OR 3.1 (1.2-8.5)**, motor-vehicle parts/supplies **4.5 (1.2-16.5)** and metal/plastic machine operators **2.0 (1.1-3.4)**. Numerous occupations were tested, estimates for some groups were imprecise, and job title is a proxy for mixed exposures. Primary abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12134533/ ; DOI 10.1097/00043764-200207000-00016. IARC's study table confirms diagnoses **1986-1989**, age/smoking/family-history adjustment: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK385452/?report=reader .

**Glioma, Zheng et al., 2001:** 375 histologically confirmed cases and 2,434 controls; the primary abstract reports male long-duration rubber/plastics (especially tires/inner tubes), newspaper work, manufacturing and repair signals, and female apparel/textile and electrical/electronic-manufacturing signals. It also reports a **non-significant** excess for broad gasoline/motor-exhaust industries. These many exploratory associations do not establish a particular agent or plant effect. Primary: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11322092/ ; DOI 10.1097/00043764-200104000-00005. The exact diagnosis period was not independently verified from full original methods in this search; secondary accounts place the broader source series in 1984-1987. Use that caveat instead of silently treating publication year as exposure year.

Both abstracts are archived as NCBI XML. A newer original Upper Midwest study included nonmetropolitan Iowa plus three other states and explicitly regarded its job associations as suggestive rather than conclusive: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4530494/ .

## 13. Reusable inputs and modeling structure

The companion `industry-manufacturing-evidence.csv` is an evidence index, not a regression-ready causal dataset. `manufacturing-exposure-measurements.csv` extracts 19 selected numerical historical HHE inputs with their summary type and units. `winnebago-2000-personal-wood-dust.csv` preserves the full 17-sample source table, and `manufacturing-input-datasets.csv` catalogs six reusable data systems. Do not treat a range endpoint, task-specific personal sample and plant-wide mean as interchangeable observations, or treat these convenience investigations as representative exposure distributions. These values can constrain historical scenarios or prioritize retrieval of complete tables; they do not estimate present Iowa worker dose.

**OSHA Chemical Exposure Health Data** offers records from 1984 forward and annual XML ZIPs. Search/join fields include establishment, state, city, ZIP, SIC/NAICS, inspection/sampling identifiers, sample date, agent, type, duration, result, units and qualifier. Closed-case restrictions delay availability. Inspections target problems and often worst cases; individual media may cover only part of a shift. Preserve `Inspection_Number`, `Sampling_Number`, `Field_Number`, `Sample_Type`, `Blank_Used`, `Eight_Hour_TWA_Calc`, `Time_Sampled`, `Qualifier` and `Unit_of_Measurement`; deduplicate media before constructing exposure episodes and do not recode nondetects to zero. Iowa State Plan and laboratory coverage must be checked before claiming a complete Iowa series. The live download endpoint was blocked (HTTP 403) in this retrieval, although official indexed documentation was readable. Dataset: https://www.osha.gov/opengov/health-samples ; dictionary: https://www.osha.gov/opengov/osha-health-samples-dataset-field-definitions .

**NOMS/NVSS mortality industry/occupation data:** usual/longest-held industry and occupation accompany public mortality files from 2020, but **Iowa is explicitly absent in 2020**. It is included in 2021's jurisdiction list; 2022 forward includes all 52 jurisdictions. These variables support occupation/industry mortality surveillance, not complete employment histories, direct exposure measurement or cancer incidence. PMRs depend on other causes of death and are not rate ratios using an employed population denominator. Codes and cancer ICD-10 endpoints must follow the selected year's layout. https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/surveillance/noms/ .

**Coding and exposure proxies:** CDC's NIOCCS offers single-record, batch and API coding of free-text jobs and industries. Keep the original text, assigned codes, classification vintage and confidence; manually check low-confidence and exposure-critical records. https://www.cdc.gov/nndss/in-action/health-data.html . O*NET 31.0 provides occupation-level work-context descriptors and task information. Its contaminant-frequency score is a broad proxy, not an agent-specific concentration or lifetime dose. Preserve O*NET-SOC version and crosswalk to SOC/Census codes rather than matching numeric strings across vintages. https://www.onetcenter.org/database.html ; https://www.onetcenter.org/dictionary/31.0/json/work_context.html ; https://www.onetonline.org/find/descriptor/result/4.C.2.b.1.d .

**Population-at-work context:** Iowa QCEW provides monthly employment/quarterly wages by industry and geography; BLS OEWS provides occupational employment estimates. These characterize employment structure, with different coverage, suppression and reference dates. Neither provides a cancer cohort denominator or each worker's historical exposure; contemporary counts cannot serve as denominators for 1980s cancers after decades of latency. Preserve geographic vintage, ownership, NAICS/SOC vintage, disclosure status and reference period. https://workforce.iowa.gov/labor-market-information/industry-employers/qcew ; https://www.bls.gov/oes/2025/may/oessrcst.htm .

Recommended analytical keys: stable facility identifier plus historical names/address; exact process/task; CAS identifier and chemical form (e.g., Cr(VI) rather than all chromium); route; sample type/duration; sampling versus reporting date; measured/modeled/inferred status; nondetect qualifier/detection limit; units; control-installation and operation intervals; occupation/industry coding vintage; cancer site/subtype and diagnosis/death year; latency/lag; smoking and other confounders; source retrieval date and page/table. Keep worker and residential pathways separate. An exposure limit comparison should retain the limit's agency, year and averaging period and should not substitute for a cancer potency model.

## Research priorities that follow

Build task/era-specific exposure histories, link them to registry outcomes under proper privacy protections, distinguish worker from community pathways, and test relevant cancer subtypes. Obtain updated sampling and control records for historical HHE locations before judging today's workplace. For community concerns, pair current/legacy emissions and vapor records with plausible exposure duration rather than drawing conclusions from county rank. A permit, chemical inventory, contamination cleanup or complaint is evidence of a different kind than an exposure measurement or an epidemiologic cancer study.

The companion CSV separates these evidence layers. Nine original PDFs and an NCBI abstract XML were successfully archived under `sources/industries/manufacturing/`; the WARN PDF download was blocked, while its indexed official text was readable. `download-log.json` records retrieval results.
